Dear Editor,

China faces a near-certain platelet shortage by 2030: our compartmental model projects a 91.5% deficit probability for platelets (95% CI 90.8–92.2) and a 100% deficit probability for cryoprecipitate, driven by a widening donor–patient ageing scissors gap in which donors aged ≥45 years rose from 12% to 28% while patients aged ≥60 years now account for 42% of clinical blood use. I am pleased to submit this work, entitled "Projected platelet shortage and red-cell supply–demand balance in an ageing China: a compartmental model validated against three city blood centres", for consideration as an Original Article in Vox Sanguinis.

The study develops the DPS-CM (Disease–Population–Supply Compartmental Model), coupling disease-specific demand (16 disease categories × 6 blood products), demographic projection via a Leslie matrix, and supply capacity with demand-elasticity feedback. The model is validated against operational data from the Shanghai, Beijing, and Shenzhen blood centres (2022–2024), with a 2024 one-step-ahead hindcast error of −1.0% for red cells and +2.4% for platelets. International comparison with Japan, Korea, and Germany identifies three highest-yield corrective levers: extending platelet shelf life from 5 to 7 days, expanding apheresis capacity, and national patient blood management.

I believe this manuscript is well suited to Vox Sanguinis because it addresses a question of direct concern to transfusion services—how demographic ageing threatens component-blood supply—using a transparent, reproducible modelling framework, and because the corrective levers (platelet shelf-life extension, apheresis expansion, PBM) are actionable in blood-centre and hospital practice. The framework is transferable to other ageing societies facing the same donor–patient scissors gap.

This manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration elsewhere. It uses only publicly available aggregate data and required no ethics approval. The complete Python implementation and input data will be made available in a public repository upon acceptance. I have read and approved the final manuscript and declare no competing interests.
